Ocean sounds for sleeping on iPhone and Android

Written by the team that builds Luten, so we are not neutral about it. Updated 15 November 2026.

The short answer

iPhone has an ocean sound built in. Open Settings, tap Accessibility, then Audio & Visual, then Background Sounds, turn it on, tap Sound and pick an ocean sound. On Android phones that use Google's Clock app, open the Bedtime tab and choose a sound under Sleep sounds, which plays a sound from another app or from your phone. In Luten, open Sleep, tap Ocean above the list and tap a sound.

On iPhone: the ocean sound in Background Sounds

You don't need an app for a plain ocean sound on iPhone. Apple's Background Sounds give ocean waves and rain as examples, and they live in the accessibility settings.

  1. Open the Settings app.
  2. Tap Accessibility, then Audio & Visual, then Background Sounds.
  3. Turn on Background Sounds.
  4. Tap the sound, then pick an ocean sound.
  5. Drag the slider to set the volume.

To keep it playing with the screen locked, leave Stop Sounds When Locked off. On iOS 26 or later, the same screen has Stop Sound with a Timer (on iOS 26 it is called Stop Sounds with a Timer), which can turn it off at a set time or after a set amount of time. These are all on Apple's page.

Background Sounds is Apple's own set, kept in Settings. If you want a choice of shores, or waves mixed with something else, you need an app. More on the shortcuts and settings in our Background Sounds guide.

On Android: Sleep sounds in the Clock app

On phones that use Google's Clock app, the Bedtime tab can start a sound for you.

  1. Open the Clock app and tap Bedtime.
  2. Under Sleep sounds, tap the current sound or Choose another sound.
  3. Pick the app and the sound you want, or a sound file on your phone.

Google's help page says sleep sounds only play when you turn them on, and that the sound comes from a media app or from your device. It doesn't list an ocean sound of its own, so you still need an ocean track in an app or a file. If your phone has a different clock app, look for a bedtime section in it.

What changes from one ocean track to another

"Ocean" covers several quite different sounds, and a title may not say which one you are getting. These are the differences worth listening for.

  • How close the shore is. Waves breaking a few steps away have a hiss and a crash in every wave. Surf heard from further back blurs into a steadier roar.
  • How regular the waves are. Each wave is a swell and a break. Some recordings have an even rhythm, others have big and small waves mixed. Listen through several waves before you decide.
  • Above or under the water. Underwater recordings are muffled and low, with no breaking waves at all.
  • What else is in it. Gulls, wind, rain, a boat or a piano are sometimes added to ocean tracks. Check the description, then listen for them.
  • Noise shaped like surf. Some tracks are noise that swells and fades in the shape of waves, rather than a recording of a beach.

Two more checks. Skip to the end of a track to hear how it finishes or joins back to the start, because a short recording on repeat can bring back the same big wave you already noticed. And play it at the volume you will really use, since a quiet track can sound thinner than it did at full volume.

If you play ocean videos on YouTube, a "no ads" title doesn't decide whether ads play. Our rain sounds page covers what YouTube's own help says about that, and the ways round it.

Ocean sounds in Luten

Luten is a sound app for iPhone and Android, with no ads. Its Sleep section has an Ocean filter that lists the sounds with names such as ocean, waves, sea or shore.

  1. On the Home screen, tap Sleep.
  2. Tap Ocean in the row of filters above the list.
  3. Tap a sound.

The list keeps the kinds above as separate tracks rather than one mix. Soothing Waves is slow waves on a quiet shore. Deep Sea is an underwater ambience. Rain and Waves mixes light rain with gentle waves. Pink Noise Waves puts pink noise over waves on sand. Ocean Brown Noise is made by Luten: brown noise that swells and recedes in the shape of surf.

To have it stop on its own, tap Sleep timer in the player and pick a length. When the time is up, a sound you tapped from a list gets quieter over about 20 seconds and then pauses. Our sleep timer guide has the details.

Questions

Is there an ocean sound built into iPhone?

Yes. Background Sounds in Settings, under Accessibility and then Audio & Visual, includes an ocean sound. You don't need to install anything.

Does Android have built-in ocean sounds?

Google's Clock app doesn't list an ocean sound of its own. On phones with that app, Bedtime can start a sleep sound, and Google says that sound comes from a media app or a file on your phone.

Can I play ocean sounds with the screen off?

On iPhone, Background Sounds keeps playing when locked as long as Stop Sounds When Locked is off. Luten is built to keep playing with the phone locked. On Android, a battery saver on some phones can stop apps running in the background, so check that setting if a sound stops.

How long should I leave ocean sounds on?

That is up to you. We don't recommend a length. If you want them to stop on their own, use a timer.

What is the difference between ocean sounds and brown noise?

An ocean recording has waves in it, each one a swell and a break. Brown noise is a steady, deep rumble with no events in it. Some tracks, such as Ocean Brown Noise in Luten, are noise shaped to rise and fall like surf. Our white noise page explains the noise colours.

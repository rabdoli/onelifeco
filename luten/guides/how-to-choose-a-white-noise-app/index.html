How to choose a white noise or sleep sounds app

Written by the team that builds Luten, so we are not neutral about it. Updated 13 November 2026.

The short answer

Try what your phone already has first: on iPhone, Background Sounds in Settings plays noise and rain with no app at all, and on Android, the Bedtime tab in Google's Clock app can play sleep sounds from a media app. If you want an app, test a few things in the first evening: it keeps playing with the screen locked, you can't hear where the sound repeats, the timer fades out, no ads cut in, and it doesn't ask you to sign up before you hear anything. Then read its privacy section on the store page.

Start with what is already on your phone

You may not need an app. Both systems have something built in, and it is already on your phone.

  • iPhone: Apple's steps: open Settings, tap Accessibility, then Audio & Visual, then Background Sounds, and turn it on. Apple lists balanced, bright and dark noise among the sounds, along with rain and ocean. Our Background Sounds guide has every step, the timer and the fixes for when it stops.
  • Android: open Google's Clock app and tap Bedtime. Under Sleep sounds, tap the current sound to choose another, then pick an app and a sound. Google's help says you can also use sounds stored on your phone.

Background Sounds can also play under your music or a podcast. If that is what you want, it may be all you need.

What to test in any white noise app

Store pages all look alike. These checks show the difference, and you can run them yourself before you rely on an app.

  1. Lock the phone. Start a sound, lock the screen and leave it. The sound should keep going, and you should see play and pause on the lock screen. On Android, a battery saver on some phones can stop apps running in the background, so check that setting if it stops.
  2. Listen for the repeat. Some noise tracks are a recording of a set length played on a loop. Listen through the point where it starts again. A click, a short gap or a sound you can pick out each time is easy to notice once you've heard it.
  3. Try the timer. Set the shortest one and wait. Does the sound fade or stop dead? Does it pause, so you can press play again, or close the app?
  4. Look for ads. On Google Play, a Contains ads label sits under the Install button or price. Google says the developer declares it. On the App Store, play a few sounds and watch for an ad between them.
  5. Check for voices and music. Some tracks open with a spoken intro or have music mixed in. Play a few right from the start.
  6. Notice what it asks first. Some apps want an account or a subscription screen before you hear anything. If an app has a subscription, read what it includes on the store page before you start one.
  7. Read the privacy section. On the App Store, scroll to App Privacy. Apple says it shows what data the app may collect, whether it is linked to you and whether it is used to track you, as reported by the developer. On Google Play, tap Data safety, then See details. Google describes it as the developer's own account of what is collected and shared.

If you aren't sure which kind of noise you want, our page on white, pink and brown noise explains the names, and the green noise guide covers a newer one.

If you are looking for a sleep sounds app

A sleep sounds app and a white noise app are mostly the same kind of app under two names. Some sleep sounds apps offer a wider mix: rain, ocean, fan and nature recordings next to plain noise. Some add music, spoken stories or guided audio. Every test above still applies. These are worth adding when the app is for bedtime:

  • What it puts in front of you. Open the app and look at the first screen. If it leads with stories or talks and you only want sound, check that you can find plain sounds without hunting for them.
  • What happens when a track ends. Some apps play one recording and stop. Others move on to the next one. Play a sound through to its end once, so you know which yours does.
  • How bright the screen is. If you open it in a dark room, look at how bright the first screen is.

If sound stops partway through the night, run through these before you give up on an app:

  1. Check the timer. A timer you set the night before may still be on.
  2. Check what else played. A call, an alarm, a video or a voice note can make a sound app pause. If it doesn't carry on by itself, press play again.
  3. On iPhone, if you use Background Sounds, look at Stop Sounds When Locked in its settings. Our Background Sounds guide has the steps.
  4. On Android, check the battery saver. On some phones it can stop apps running in the background. Look in the app's battery settings on your phone.

Which way to go

  • You want noise and nothing else: start with Background Sounds on iPhone, or the Bedtime sounds in Google's Clock app on Android.
  • You want it under your music: Background Sounds, with Use When Media is Playing turned on.
  • You want more kinds of sound, a timer that fades and no ads: try an app, and run the tests above on it in the first evening. Luten is one to try.

Questions

Is there a white noise app built into iPhone?

Yes, in a way. Background Sounds is a setting rather than an app: Settings, Accessibility, Audio & Visual, Background Sounds. Apple lists balanced, bright and dark noise among its sounds.

Does Android have built-in white noise?

Google's Clock app has Sleep sounds in its Bedtime tab, which plays a sound from an app you choose or a sound stored on your phone.

How can I tell if a white noise app has ads?

On Google Play, look for the Contains ads label under the Install button or price. On the App Store, play a few sounds and see. Luten runs no third-party ads.

Can a white noise app play while I listen to music?

Some sound apps pause your music when they start, and Luten does. On iPhone, Background Sounds can play under music: turn on Use When Media is Playing in its settings.

Will a white noise app keep playing when the screen locks?

It should, and it is worth checking in the first evening. Luten keeps playing with the phone locked. On Android, a battery saver on some phones can stop background apps, so check that setting if sound stops.

What is the difference between a white noise app and a sleep sounds app?

The names overlap. Some sleep sounds apps go beyond noise and add rain, ocean, fan and nature recordings, and sometimes music or spoken stories. The same tests apply to both: locked playback, the loop, the timer, ads and voices.

Why does my sleep sounds app keep stopping?

Check a timer left on from before, and whether a call, alarm or video paused it. On Android, a battery saver on some phones can stop background apps, so check the app's battery settings. On iPhone, Background Sounds has a Stop Sounds When Locked setting.

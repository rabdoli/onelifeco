The one paragraph version

Many AI note takers are built around a bot that joins your video call, a web dashboard and a monthly allowance of minutes. Cassette is a cassette deck in your pocket. Press record for a meeting, a lecture, an interview or a voice memo, and when you stop, it writes a transcript with timestamps, a short summary, the highlights, the action items with owners and due dates, and a follow-up email for the people who came up. The transcript comes from Apple speech recognition and the notes are written on the iPhone itself, so a recording never has to reach a server of ours. Recording is free and unlimited, with no minute meter.

Why it exists

A lot of the conversations worth keeping happen in rooms rather than on video calls, and what people need from them afterwards is small: what was decided, who is doing what, and by when. Cassette was built around that moment after the meeting. It takes the most familiar recording interface there is, a tape deck with keys you press, and puts the writing up behind the stop key instead of inside a dashboard.

The design brief was an object rather than an app: piano keys that travel and latch, a tape counter, VU meters, side A and B and a speed knob, with haptics on every control and key sounds cut from a public-domain recording of a real cassette player. Press Mark while you record and that moment leads the highlights later.

What is actually different

The writing happens on the iPhone

Transcripts come from Apple speech recognition, on the device wherever the iPhone supports the language; otherwise that recording may be sent to Apple to transcribe. The notes come from Apple's on-device language model on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, and from a simpler writer that also runs on the device on other iPhones. One Life runs no server that processes recordings. A tape is only uploaded if its owner signs in with Premium, to back it up to their own account.

No minute meter

Recording is free and unlimited on every plan, however long the meeting. The free plan writes transcripts and notes for 5 tapes a month; Premium writes up every tape and backs up the shelf.

Built for the follow-up

Every tape gets an email draft addressed to the people who were named, with a subject, a recap and the action items, ready to paste into Mail. Any tab can be copied in one tap or saved as a PDF or a Word document.

A lifetime option

$79.99 once, no renewal, alongside the weekly, monthly and annual plans.

Feature summary

  • Records meetings, lectures, interviews and voice memos, and keeps recording with the screen locked; audio is saved every few seconds as it records
  • Transcript with timestamps in the iPhone's language; tap any line to replay that moment, with the names people say picked out
  • Notes with a summary, highlights led by the moments marked while recording, and action items with owners and due dates
  • A follow-up email draft for every tape, plus copy, PDF and Word export
  • A shelf of tapes, searchable by title, by the people in them or by any word that was said
  • Playback from half speed to double speed
  • Optional account with Sign in with Apple, Google or email; backup with Premium; delete a tape or the whole account inside the app

Positioning notes for writers

Cassette records what the iPhone's microphone hears. It does not join video calls as a bot, and a phone call pauses the recording rather than capturing it. Recording laws differ from place to place, and Cassette's Terms ask people to get consent from the people they record.

Transcripts are machine-written and can contain mistakes, especially with poor audio or people talking over each other, and the notes are only as good as the transcript under them. The company would rather that be said plainly than have Cassette described as a perfect record of a meeting.
